# Ad Full-Case Proposal

## Tear the brief apart before anything else

- Read the whole brief first and list what it actually gives you: product, budget, timeline, KPI, competitors, target audience. Flag what is missing instead of guessing it.
- Decide the one job the campaign must do: sell product, build brand awareness, or win recruitment or dealers. If the goal is unclear, stop and confirm it, because everything after it is built on this choice.
- Restate the brief in one paragraph in your own words and check it with the client before you do any creative work.

## Research in three blocks

- Study what the market and the competitors are saying, and write down where they all sound the same.
- Find the real consumer pain, not the assumed one, and note the exact words people use for it when you can.
- List what the brand itself can credibly say, and drop any claim the product cannot support.
- Deliver research as conclusions, not as a pile of material. Each conclusion gets one line of evidence behind it.

## Set the strategy in one sentence

- Write the strategy as one sentence that answers three things: who it is for, why they should believe it, and how it differs from the competitors.
- Lock the strategy before any creative work starts. If a creative idea does not follow from this sentence, cut it or rewrite the sentence.
- Say what the campaign will not do, in one line, so the scope stays honest.

## Build the Big Idea

- State the Big Idea in one sentence a stranger can repeat back to you.
- Give it three parts: a core creative concept, a key visual, and a slogan or line.
- Show how the idea splits into different deliverables, such as a main film, cutdowns, posters, and social posts. An idea that only works as one film is not finished yet.
- Keep the idea traceable to the strategy sentence. If you cannot draw the line, the idea is decoration.

## Write the proposal outline

- Build the proposal in this order, and keep each section short enough to present out loud:
  1. Brief, restated in one paragraph.
  2. Research conclusions, three blocks, one page at most.
  3. Strategy, one sentence plus the three answers behind it.
  4. Big Idea, key visual, and slogan.
  5. Creative extensions, what each deliverable is and where it runs.
  6. Media and launch plan, channels, rhythm, and budget split.
  7. Production plan, from script to delivery, with the shoot days named.
  8. Schedule, with client review points marked.
  9. Quote, broken down so the client can see what each part costs.
  10. Measurement, what success looks like and how it will be reviewed.
- Expect two to three rounds of revision after the client sees it. Note which parts are fixed and which are open to change, so a revision does not reopen the strategy.

## Plan media and launch

- Decide where the work runs, how much goes to each channel, and in what rhythm, before you promise a result.
- Set the measurement targets before launch, so the review afterwards has something to judge against.
- Check material versions, links, and tracking codes before anything goes live. A wrong link on launch day is a production failure, not a media problem.

## Hand off to production

- Production starts only after the client signs off on the proposal. Do not shoot on a verbal maybe.
- Hand over the locked script, the shot list, the casting, the locations, and the schedule as one package, so the shoot can be planned without re-deciding the creative.
- Use the storyboard skill for the shot list: hard cuts, one shot size per shot, expression and body state written into every row, and a check against the table before delivery.

## Monitor, optimise, and close out

- Watch exposure, clicks, conversion, and comment sentiment after launch, and move budget toward what works.
- Swap out weak material early instead of defending it.
- Close the job with a short report: what the data said, what worked, what did not, and what to do next time. The close-out report is the next job's door-opener, so write it as if the same client will read it first.

## Check the proposal before delivery

- Check that no number, claim, or quote in the proposal is invented. Flag anything unverified in plain words instead of smoothing it over.
- Check that every creative choice traces back to the strategy sentence, and that the budget and the schedule are both in the document.
- Read the proposal out loud once. If a section cannot be said in plain words, rewrite it before the client sees it.
